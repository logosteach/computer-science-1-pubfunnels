The For Loop and the range() Function

In this lesson, we will explore the for loop and the range() function in Python. The for loop allows us to iterate over a sequence of items. A for loop is best used when you have a sequence that is a fixed size. In other words, you know exactly the number of iterations you want to perform. The range() function is often used in conjunction with the for loop, and for this reason it is introduced formally here.

The Lord directed Joshua and the children of Israel to march around the city of Jericho a fixed number of times. Once each day for six days, they were to march around the city. Then, on the seventh day, they were to march around the city seven times. This is the for loop in action. The Lord had His own purposes for this sequence of events to unfold in the life of His people, through whom would come our blessed savior Jesus Christ. Do you wonder sometimes at why the Lord gave the orders for the Israelites to march around the city of Jericho in this way? We can be assured that the Lord had His reasons, and that He is sovereignly working all things together for good for all those that love Him and are called according to His purpose (Romans 8:28).

Objectives

By the end of the lesson, learners will be able to:

  • Write and understand the syntax of a for loop.

  • Use the range() function with start, stop, and step parameters.

  • Iterate over sequences such as strings and simple lists.

  • Explain when to use a for loop versus a while loop.

  • Recognize that just as the for loop allows us to move through a known sequence step by step, God leads us one step at a time through life, guiding our path with His Word (Psalm 119:105; Proverbs 3:5-6).
