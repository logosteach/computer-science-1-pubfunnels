Lesson 001 - Order of Operations in Python — Talking Points

Key talking points and discussion notes for teaching order of operations (PEMDAS), parentheses, evaluating expressions, and common precedence mistakes.

Main Teaching Points

  • PEMDAS in Python: Python evaluates exponents, then *, /, //, %, then + and −. Same-level * and / go left to right; ** is right-associative.

  • Parentheses are clarity tools: They force order and document intent. Teach students to use them for averages and multi-step formulas.

  • Predict before you run: Have students evaluate by hand first, then verify with print().

  • Common bugs: Missing parentheses before division; assuming + and * have equal priority; misreading **.

  • Patience parallel: Some steps must complete before others—connect carefully to John 16:12 without overstretching the metaphor.

Discussion & Talking Points

  • Question 1: Why does 2 + 3 * 4 equal 14 and not 20?

  • Question 2: Rewrite an average formula with and without parentheses. What goes wrong without them?

  • Question 3: What does “right-associative” mean for 2 ** 3 ** 2?

  • Question 4: How can you make a complex expression more readable for a classmate?

  • Question 5: How does waiting for the right step in an expression help us talk about waiting on the Lord’s timing (John 16:12)?

Biblical Integration

Just as some operations must wait until others are completed, Jesus told His disciples that there were things they were not yet ready to bear. Waiting on the Lord’s timing is part of walking with Him.

Scripture (NKJV):
“I still have many things to say to you, but you cannot bear them now.” (John 16:12, NKJV)
