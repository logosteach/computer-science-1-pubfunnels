Lesson 010 - The input() Function — Talking Points

Key talking points and discussion notes for teaching reading user input, storing it in variables, converting types, and simple interactive programs.

Main Teaching Points

  • input always returns str: Even when the user types digits.

  • Prompt strings: Clear questions help users (and students) succeed.

  • Convert early: int/float before math.

  • Interactive pattern: ask → store → convert → compute → print.

  • Prayer parallel: Bring needs to God (Phil 4; Matt 6:8)—relationship, not informing an unaware deity.

Discussion & Talking Points

  • Question 1: What type does input() always return?

  • Question 2: Why do you often wrap input() with int() or float()?

  • Question 3: Write a 3-line program pattern: ask for name, greet with an f-string.

  • Question 4: What error appears if int(input()) receives "hello"?

  • Question 5: How does bringing requests to God in prayer differ from typing into a program—and how is the invitation still a helpful parallel?

Biblical Integration

input() invites us to bring values into a program so they can be used. God invites us to bring our needs to Him in prayer. He already knows, yet He calls us to ask with thanksgiving because we are in relationship with Him through Christ.

“And my God shall supply all your need according to His riches in glory by Christ Jesus.” (Philippians 4:19, NKJV)

“…For your Father knows the things you have need of before you ask Him.” (Matthew 6:8, NKJV)
