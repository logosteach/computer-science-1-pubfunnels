### Self-Grading & Engagement Reflection  
*(This is not about points — it is about becoming more aware of how you solve problems.)*

Honestly answer the questions below. There are no right or wrong answers, only opportunities for growth.

**1. Process Awareness**  
- Did I plan my nested structure on paper or in comments before I started coding, or did I jump straight into writing code?  
- How many times did I run the program and change input values while building it?

**2. Branch Coverage**  
- Can I confidently reach **every major access outcome** (gate only, Levels 1-2, Levels 1-4, full access, and denied without A) by changing my two inputs?  
- Which combination of cards was the hardest for me to get right? Why?

**3. Debugging & Persistence**  
- When something didn’t work the first time, what did I do? (e.g., added print statements, re-read the requirements, asked for help, took a short break, etc.)  
- Did I ever feel stuck? What helped me get unstuck?

**4. Design Decisions**  
- Why did I choose the particular order of my nested `if` statements?  
- If I had more time, what would I improve about my code’s readability or structure?

**5. Faithful Stewardship of Learning**  
- In what ways did this lab require careful, layered thinking — the kind of thinking that also helps us follow God’s guidance through complex circumstances (Proverbs 16:9; Romans 8:28)?  
- Did I approach this challenge with patience, honesty, and a desire to truly understand, or did I mainly try to “get it done”?

**Final Self-Check**  
I have:  
- [ ] A working program that correctly requires Key Card A for any entry  
- [ ] Nested `if`/`else` logic that I can explain to someone else  
- [ ] Tested multiple combinations (including higher cards without A)  
- [ ] Written thoughtful answers to the reflection questions above  

If you can check most of these boxes with integrity, you have meaningfully engaged the learning process. Well done!
