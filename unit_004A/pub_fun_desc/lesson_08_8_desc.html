Practical, Real World Problems

In this lesson, we will apply the concepts of conditionals to solve practical, real-world problems. This will help us practice with what we have been learning about conditionals and help synthesize the concepts together. You may not know this but the Greek word for Acts is "Praxis". Praxis means "practical application". Therefore, the Acts of the Apostles is really the "Practical Application of the Apostles".

Objectives

By the end of the lesson, learners will be able to:

  • Solve practical, real-world problems using conditional logic.

  • Combine conditionals with input(), variables, and previous concepts.

  • Build small interactive programs that make decisions.

  • Apply conditional logic confidently in increasingly complex scenarios.

  • Reflect on how the ability to make decisions in code reflects the God-given gift of choice and the importance of using that gift wisely for His glory (1 Corinthians 10:31).

Computer Lab: Smart Theme Park Ticket & Access Advisor

Overview:

This engaging computer lab helps students practice everything learned in Unit 4A on conditional logic. Students build a realistic ticket pricing and ride access system for Adventure Kingdom Theme Park.

Key Skills Practiced:

- Multi-branch if/elif/else chains for ticket pricing

- Ternary operators for quick ride access decisions

- Best practices for clean, readable, and maintainable code

- Debugging common pitfalls (e.g., = vs ==, indentation, missing colons)

- Testing all decision branches with multiple input combinations

Biblical Integration:

Students reflect on how ordered, wise decision-making in programming mirrors biblical calls to do things decently and in order (1 Corinthians 14:40 NKJV) and to ponder the path of their feet (Proverbs 4:26-27 NKJV).

Lab Structure:

- Guided steps with starter code and test cases

- Independent integration challenge

- Reflection questions and bonus extensions

- Complete solutions provided for instructors

Deliverable:

Students save their work as theme_park_lab.py and discuss their design choices.

Perfect for reinforcing conditional logic before moving to loops!

Labs, Practice Files and Assessments are developed in collaboration with the Grok AI assistant under instructor supervision and review.

Anchored in Christ

Not At All Complex

We have talked a lot in this unit about complexity. Decisions, decisions, decisions. We have focused on if/elif/else blocks, ternary operators, and truthy and falsy values. We have seen that living the Christian life can be very complex, but we are not alone.

Yet there is something about our life in Christ that is not at all complex. It is actually quite straightforward. One simple question, if we ask ourselves honestly, can lay bare the reality of this truth: What is my motivation, at the root level, for serving Christ?

Do I see Him for the treasure that He is? Is my inner heart motivated by the thought of winning “brownie points” with Him, or do I just like being on the “winning team”? Or—and remember, dear one, this is something we all need to grow into—am I motivated by sheer love for Him and thankfulness? Because at the end of the day, a review of the Scriptures tells us that is exactly what He wants. He loves us because He loves us. Are we motivated not by a perfect love, but by a genuine love that simply wants to seek Him and love Him for who He is?

Philippians 3:7-8 (NKJV)

“But what things were gain to me, these I have counted loss for Christ. Yet indeed I also count all things loss for the excellence of the knowledge of Christ Jesus my Lord, for whom I have suffered the loss of all things, and count them as rubbish, that I may gain Christ.”
