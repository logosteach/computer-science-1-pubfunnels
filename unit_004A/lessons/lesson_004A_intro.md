# Conditionals Narration

Today we’re learning how to control the flow of our Python programs using conditional statements—`if`, `elif`, and `else`. These tools let your code make intelligent decisions based on conditions, just as we face choices every day in life.

## Compare

Think about Joshua 24:15 (NKJV):

> “And if it seems evil to you to serve the Lord, choose for yourselves this day whom you will serve, whether the gods which your fathers served... But as for me and my house, we will serve the Lord.”

Joshua presents clear conditions and branches—much like an `if`/`elif`/`else` chain. The program (or person) evaluates options in order and follows the correct path. When we structure our code properly—with correct indentation and colons—we create reliable, orderly decision-making that leads to the desired outcome. In the same way, ordering our lives with God’s priorities first produces blessing and purpose.

## Contrast

Now consider Proverbs 14:12 (NKJV):

> “There is a way that seems right to a man, but its end is the way of death.”

Here we see the danger of faulty logic. If we skip proper conditions, use the wrong order (like putting `elif` before `if`), or fail to test thoroughly, our program crashes or produces wrong results. Similarly, when we rely on our own limited understanding instead of God’s truth, what looks like a good path can lead to spiritual dead ends. Proper conditional logic in code teaches us the importance of discernment, testing every “if,” and choosing the narrow way that leads to life (see also Matthew 7:13-14).

As you write your conditionals, remember: just as Christ is the perfect “if” in our salvation—**if** we trust in Him alone, then we have eternal life—let your code reflect that same clarity, order, and wisdom. Master these structures, and you’ll not only write better programs but walk more faithfully in the decisions of life.
